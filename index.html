Rachel K. E. Bellamy, Ph.D.

I help organizations build AI that people can understand, oversee and trust.

rkebconsulting@gmail.com Google Scholar LinkedIn CV

I am an independent consultant in AI safety and responsible AI. For 28 years at IBM Research I worked where AI meets the people who build, govern and depend on it. I led the human-centered design of IBM's Trusted AI toolkits (AI Fairness 360, AI Explainability 360 and AI FactSheets) and chaired the Exploratory Computer Science Council, which set direction for IBM's long-range computing research.

My view: most AI risk shows up at the human interface, and much of it is indirect. People ask for things imprecisely or ambiguously, and an AI agent fills the gaps with goals of its own. A request that looks harmless can set off secondary effects nobody thought through: steps taken along the way, side effects on other systems or people, consequences that surface only later. On top of that, people over-trust models, cannot see what an agent is doing, and often have no practical way to intervene. Safety needs good models, but it also needs oversight that real people can carry out, including help in anticipating what a request might set in motion. That is the problem I help teams solve, now especially for agentic and generative AI.

I currently consult for IBM Research and for Chematiqs, a start-up building AI assistants that help chemists find alternatives to PFAS "forever chemicals".

Selected work

Trusted AI toolkits: fairness, explainability and documentation

IBM Research · 2018 – 2022

Led human-centered design of AI Fairness 360, AI Explainability 360 and AI FactSheets, tools that gave practitioners practical ways to detect bias, explain models and document AI systems. The fairness and explainability toolkits became the leading open-source tools in their area, and IBM transferred them to the Linux Foundation in 2020 for independent governance; AI Fairness 360 is still downloaded tens of thousands of times a month. FactSheets became the core of IBM's AI governance product, and a study by the European Commission's Joint Research Centre (Hupont et al., IEEE Computer, 2023) rated it the most complete of six documentation approaches against the EU AI Act.

AI assistants that support, not replace, expert judgment

IBM Research and Chematiqs · 2023 – present

Designing agentic and generative AI assistants for chemical discovery, with a focus on keeping chemists in control of what the agent proposes and why. The work targets safer alternatives to PFAS. Co-inventor on U.S. Patent 12,235,933 (2025), Increasing Trust Formation and Reducing Oversight Costs for Autonomous Agents.

Setting direction for exploratory research

Chair, Exploratory Computer Science Council, IBM Research · 2019 – 2025

Chaired the council that selected and curated IBM Research's portfolio of exploratory science projects, in areas including artificial general intelligence, biologically-inspired computation, socio-technical systems and distributed computing. Projects the council backed include the Natural Language Interaction Protocol (NLIP), led by Dinesh Verma, now recognized by Ecma International as a family of standards for AI agent ecosystems; and Justin Weisz and colleagues' early design principles for generative AI applications and research on the future of human-AI work.
