The problem I work on

Most AI risk shows up at the human interface, and much of it is indirect. People ask for things imprecisely, and an AI agent fills the gaps with goals of its own. A request that looks harmless can set off effects nobody anticipated:

  • Steps along the way: subgoals and actions the person never asked for.
  • Effects on others: side effects on other systems, data or people.
  • Later consequences: problems that surface only after the task looks done.

Meanwhile people over-trust models, cannot see what an agent is doing, and often have no practical way to step in. Good models are necessary but not sufficient. Safety also needs oversight that real people can carry out, including help in anticipating what a request might set in motion.

How I can help

Agent oversight review

For teams building or deploying AI agents

A fixed-scope review of your agent: where it can act beyond what users asked, what side effects it can have, what users can see and where they can step in. You get a prioritized list of fixes and an oversight design.

Human-agent interaction research

For AI labs and product research teams

Design and run studies of how people specify tasks to agents, supervise them and correct them, including how to detect and measure unintended secondary effects.

Governance and documentation programs

For organizations preparing for AI regulation

Set up responsible AI governance and documentation, such as fact sheets and model documentation, that works for users, auditors and regulators, including the EU AI Act.

Strategic advisory

For safety, policy and research leaders

Ongoing advice on research and product direction, which bets to make and how to evaluate them, drawing on six years choosing IBM Research's exploratory portfolio.

Projects the council backed include a project applying generative AI to computer communication networks, which eventually led to the Natural Language Interaction Protocol (NLIP), led by Dinesh Verma, now recognized by Ecma International and by ISO (International Standards Organization) as a family of standards for AI agent ecosystems; and a project, led by Justin Weisz, articulating early design principles for generative AI applications and for working with AI agents.

Track record: trustworthy AI tools adopted at scale

IBM Research · 2018 – 2022

Led human-centered design of AI Fairness 360, AI Explainability 360 and AI FactSheets, tools that gave practitioners practical ways to detect bias, explain models and document AI systems. The fairness and explainability toolkits became the leading open-source tools in their area, and IBM transferred them to the Linux Foundation in 2020 for independent governance; AI Fairness 360 is still downloaded tens of thousands of times a month. FactSheets became the core of IBM's AI governance product, and a study by the European Commission's Joint Research Centre (Hupont et al., IEEE Computer, 2023) rated it the most complete of six documentation approaches against the EU AI Act.
